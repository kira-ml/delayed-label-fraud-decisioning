# Baseline Audit and Ablation

_Generated by `python -m src.evaluation.ablation` on the frozen split. Every number below traces to `reports/ablation.json`._

## Why this report exists

The V1 headline compares the cost-sensitive policy against a 0.5 cut-off. At a ~1.1% base rate, a 0.5 cut-off is almost approve-all, so that comparison overstates the value of the policy. This report asks the harder question: **does the policy beat the best simple rule a competent team would actually deploy?**

The fair baselines are cost-optimal thresholds tuned on the validation window and applied unchanged to the test window. The test-tuned oracle is a diagnostic upper bound and is not a deployable baseline.

## Uncertainty on the fair comparison

- Paired bootstrap, 2,000 iterations, seed 42
- Cost difference vs fair two-threshold: [0.001834, 0.002475]
- Advantage vs fair two-threshold: **22.29%** [19.71%, 24.95%]
- Month-stratified bootstrap (robustness): [19.55%, 24.81%]
- Advantage vs LGBM + 0.5: **57.91%**; the pre-registered interval is [53.95%, 62.13%] in `reports/bootstrap.md`. A paired ratio bootstrap gives [56.08%, 59.61%] as a diagnostic.

## Interpretation

The policy still beats the best simple rule by **22.29%**, above the pre-registered 5% effect-size threshold, and the 95% CI excludes zero. The headline against a 0.5 cut-off is **57.91%**; most of that gap is threshold placement, not modeling.

Two design choices carry the rest: adding the review action (31.02% over approve/block only) and conditioning the loss on the amount (23.87% over a constant loss). Those are the contributions worth reporting.

Even a global threshold tuned on the test window itself (0.009129) is worse than the deployed policy (0.007491); hindsight does not close the gap. The gain is amount conditioning, not threshold luck.

## Limitations

- Thresholds are tuned on the validation window; a different validation window could move them.
- Single chronological split and one cost matrix, as in V1.
- The paired bootstrap resamples rows; it likely understates temporal dependence. The month-stratified CI is a partial check.
- The test-tuned oracle is not deployable; it bounds how much a global-threshold rule could gain from hindsight.
- BAF is synthetic; these are not production estimates.

## Verdict

**The result survives the fair baseline.** Report both numbers: 57.9% vs a 0.5 cut-off, and 22.3% vs the best cost-optimal threshold. The second number is the one to defend.
